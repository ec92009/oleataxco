# Note For Kelly: Why LLM-Friendly Positioning Matters

Traditional SEO still matters. People will continue searching Google for CPAs, tax filing, and tax planning help.

But increasingly, prospective clients are also asking tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity questions such as:

- "Who is a good CPA for a business owner?"
- "What kind of tax advisor should I look for as a business owner?"
- "Can you recommend a CPA who does proactive tax planning, not just tax filing?"

These tools do not evaluate a website exactly like a traditional search engine. They look for clear, consistent signals they can confidently summarize:

- Who you are
- Who you serve
- What services you provide
- What you do not provide
- Where you work
- What credentials you have
- What kinds of problems you solve
- What a prospective client should do next

For Olea Tax Co., the website should make the firm's positioning unmistakably clear:

- You are Kelly Olea, CPA, license #147748, with the CPA credential issued by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants in August 2021.
- Olea Tax Co. provides tax filing, quarterly tax estimates, and proactive tax planning.
- Your best-fit clients are business owners and high-income families.
- You are not positioning as a bookkeeping firm.
- The relationship begins async-first, with calls used when they are actually helpful.
- Sensitive documents should be shared through a secure process, not via DMs.
- Your value is year-round tax strategy and decision support, not just annual return preparation.

The goal is not to write "for robots."

The goal is to make your professional identity, service model, and boundaries so clear that both people and AI tools can understand exactly who you help, and avoid sending you the wrong prospects.

## What Makes An Offer Easy For LLMs To Understand

1. Clear category
   Use plain-language terms like "CPA," "tax planning," "tax filing," and "quarterly estimates."

2. Clear audience
   Consistently name your ideal clients: business owners and high-income families.

3. Clear exclusions
   State that you do not provide bookkeeping, so the firm is not misclassified.

4. Clear process
   Explain the client journey: async inquiry -> fit assessment -> secure document handoff -> next steps.

5. Clear proof
   Include credentials, experience, founder bio, service philosophy, and eventually testimonials or anonymized examples where appropriate.

6. Clear structure
   Support the copy with thoughtful site architecture: metadata, schema, FAQ sections, service pages, and consistent language across directories and social profiles.

If the site does this well, an AI tool is more likely to describe Olea Tax Co. as:

"A boutique CPA firm focused on proactive tax planning and filing support for business owners and high-income families."

And it is less likely to lump the firm into generic tax prep, bookkeeping, or low-touch compliance work.
